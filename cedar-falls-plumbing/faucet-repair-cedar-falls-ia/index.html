    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in Cedar Falls

    Weak or sputtering flow is usually caused by mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge. We remove and clean or replace the aerator, flush the lines and check the cartridge for scale blockage. On pull-down faucets, the spray head and check valves can also clog. If flow is low at every fixture, we test whole-house water pressure.

    Compression Faucet Repair in Cedar Falls

    Compression faucets, often found in older Cedar Falls homes, are known for their separate hot and cold handles. When these faucets leak, it's typically due to worn-out rubber washers or packing. Our team is skilled in disassembling these classic fixtures, replacing the necessary seals and washers, and reassembling them to stop leaks for good. We respect the character of older homes and make sure these repairs are done carefully.

    Outdoor Hose Bib Repair in Cedar Falls

    Outdoor spigots take a beating from sun and weather. Frost goes deep into the ground across Iowa, so water and sewer lines must be buried safely below the frost line. We repair leaking packing nuts, replace worn washers and install new frost-free hose bibs when necessary. We also add vacuum breakers to meet backflow prevention requirements. A leaking hose bib can waste a lot of water over a long season of yard use.

    Faucet Repair Versus Replacement in Cedar Falls, IA

    A cartridge repair often costs well under the price of a new faucet and can add years of life. However, if the faucet body is cracked, parts are discontinued or it has needed several repairs, replacement may be better value. We give you both prices so you can choose. Many brands like Moen and Delta offer lifetime warranties on cartridges, which can lower your repair cost.

    Faucet Handle Repair & Replacement in Cedar Falls, IA

    A loose, broken, or stripped faucet handle makes it hard to control water flow and temperature. Over time, daily use can wear down the internal mechanisms or cause the handle itself to break. We offer expert repair for various handle types, from single-handle mixers to separate hot and cold handles. If a repair isn't feasible, we can recommend and install a suitable replacement handle that matches your existing fixture and style, extending its lifespan.

    Leaking Faucet Handle and Base Repair in Cedar Falls, IA

    Water seeping around the handle or pooling at the base points to failed O-rings or a loose mounting nut. If left alone, the leak can soak into the cabinet below and damage the sink base. We replace O-rings, tighten mounting hardware and reseal the base. We also check the deck for water stains.

    Ball Faucet Repair in Cedar Falls, IA

    Ball faucets, recognizable by their single lever that moves over a rounded cap, are common in many kitchens. They use a rotating ball joint to control water flow and temperature. If your ball faucet is leaking from the spout or base, or if the handle feels loose, it's probably time for a repair. We can replace the spring-loaded rubber seats and O-rings, which are usually the culprits, restoring smooth operation and preventing drips.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Cedar Falls

    Most faucet repairs cost $125 to $300, including the cartridge or seals and labor. Complex kitchen or touchless faucet repairs can run $200 to $400. We quote a firm price before any work starts.

    A worn cartridge, washer or valve seat is the usual culprit. Hard water scale makes these parts wear out faster, so drips are common in many homes.

    Water across much of Iowa comes from groundwater and runs hard to very hard, so scale buildup in water heaters and fixtures is common. Hard water contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or prompt repair important.

    If the faucet body is solid and parts are available, a repair is usually worth it. If it is corroded, cracked or discontinued, a new faucet is often the better choice.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If a special part must be ordered, we might need a second short visit.

    Common signs include a persistent drip or leak, difficulty turning the handle, low water pressure, strange noises when the faucet is on, or visible corrosion around the base or spout.
