    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in Marion

    CIPP lining typically costs $80 to $250 per foot, and pipe bursting often runs $60 to $200 per foot plus access pits. A typical residential sewer line usually totals $4,000 to $15,000 depending on length and condition.

    CIPP (cured-in-place pipe) lining involves inserting a new liner inside the existing pipe to create a pipe within a pipe. Pipe bursting, on the other hand, involves breaking apart the old pipe while pulling a new pipe into its place, which leaves you with a completely new pipe.

    We use liners and resins from established manufacturers such as Perma-Liner and similar systems rated for sewer use. We choose materials based on pipe size and condition.

    Trenchless pipe repairs, particularly CIPP lining and pipe bursting using materials like epoxy resins and HDPE pipe, are designed to last for 50 years or more. These materials are very resistant to corrosion, root intrusion, and chemical breakdown, offering a very durable solution.

    A properly installed epoxy liner is designed to last 50 years or more. HDPE pipe used in bursting has a similar long service life.

    Most trenchless pipe repair projects, especially CIPP lining, can be completed in one to three days. Pipe bursting might take a bit longer, depending on the pipe length and complexity. This is significantly faster than traditional methods, which can take weeks due to excavation and restoration.

    Most common pipe materials, including cast iron, clay, PVC, and concrete, can be repaired using trenchless methods. However, the condition of the existing pipe is a factor. If a pipe is completely collapsed or badly misaligned, pipe bursting might be a more suitable trenchless option than lining. Our inspection will identify the best approach.

    While trenchless methods are very versatile, they are not suitable for all types of pipe damage. Severely collapsed pipes, pipes with significant offsets, or pipes that have suffered extreme structural failure may still require traditional excavation. A professional camera inspection from KFR Plumbing will determine what is possible.
