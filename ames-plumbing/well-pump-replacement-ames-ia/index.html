    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Ames

    The cost of well pump replacement in Ames, IA usually ranges from $1,500 to $3,500, though it can vary. Factors influencing the price include the type of pump, well depth, horsepower and any additional components needed like pressure tanks or wiring upgrades. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after a thorough assessment.

    KFR Plumbing installs and services a wide range of reputable well pump brands known for their durability and performance. These commonly include Grundfos, Franklin Electric, Goulds Pumps and Sta-Rite. We can recommend the best brand and model to suit your well's needs.

    In many areas, permits may be required for significant plumbing work like well pump replacement, especially if it involves changing the well system's electrical or structural components. KFR Plumbing will help you navigate any permitting in Ames, IA so your installation is fully compliant with local regulations.

    Regular maintenance, such as checking your well's water level, keeping the pressure tank air charge correct and fixing any leaks promptly, can extend your pump's life. Avoiding excessive water use and installing a good quality sediment filter can also reduce wear and tear on the pump.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, typically less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has specific applications and benefits.

    It is often a good idea to consider replacing your pressure tank at the same time as your well pump, especially if both are of similar age. A new pressure tank can improve the efficiency and lifespan of your new pump by reducing cycling. It also cuts labor costs to do both jobs at once.

    Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is typically 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water. Deep well pumps, or submersible pumps, are placed directly in the water near the bottom of the well and push water up, suited for depths over 25 feet. Choosing the correct type is key for efficiency and performance.

    If your pressure tank is old or waterlogged, replacing it at the same time can protect the new pump. A failed tank causes rapid short cycling, which is one of the most common reasons submersible motors burn out early.
