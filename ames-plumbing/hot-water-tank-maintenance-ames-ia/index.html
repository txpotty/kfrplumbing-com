    Gas Burner and Venting Tune-Up in Ames, IA

    On gas tanks we clean the burner, inspect the thermocouple or flame sensor and look for soot, rust flakes or scorching around the combustion chamber. We check the draft hood and vent connector for proper slope and secure joints. Dust and lint from garages can block the flame arrestor screen on newer FVIR models, which causes the pilot to drop out. A clean burner gives a steady blue flame and lower gas bills.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Ames, IA

    The anode rod, often called the sacrificial rod, is vital for preventing corrosion inside your hot water tank. Water chemistry varies from one neighborhood to the next, and it affects the rod's lifespan. Our technicians inspect the anode rod for wear and recommend replacement if it's badly corroded, typically every 3 to 5 years. Replacing a worn anode rod is a cost-effective way to protect your tank from rust and leaks, sparing you a full tank replacement.

    Electric Element and Thermostat Service in Ames

    Electric tanks rely on upper and lower heating elements that get crusted with scale in hard water. We test each element with a meter, check the thermostats and look for scorched wires at the terminal block. If an element has failed we replace it with the matching wattage and voltage. Plenty of families who think they need a new heater just need a lower element and a flush.

    Over time, minerals and sediment settle at the bottom of hot water tanks, reducing efficiency and potentially leading to premature failure. Our maintenance includes a thorough flushing of the tank to remove these deposits. This process helps your tank heat water more efficiently and quietly, a vital step for any hot water tank in Ames, IA, where mineral content in the water is a common concern.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Ames

    The cost for hot water tank maintenance in Ames, IA usually ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age and specific services required like anode rod replacement. We provide a detailed quote before any work starts.

    Watch for slower recovery, rusty or cloudy hot water, noises, moisture around the base or a relief valve that drips. Any of these means it is time for an inspection visit.

    Regular, professional maintenance can frequently be a condition for keeping your manufacturer's warranty valid. Check your warranty documentation, but generally, proof of annual service can help keep your warranty intact.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to inefficient operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service prevents these costly and dangerous issues.

    Water across much of Iowa comes from groundwater and runs hard to very hard, so scale buildup in water heaters and fixtures is common. Mineral content adds to sediment buildup in hot water tanks, which reduces efficiency and can damage heating elements or tank linings over time. Regular flushing is particularly important in hard water areas.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which matters most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it lengthens the lifespan of your unit.

    While some basic steps like draining a small amount of water can be done by a homeowner, professional hot water tank maintenance involves professional tools and experience to properly inspect electrical or gas components, anode rods and pressure relief valves. It is best left to a professional to protect safety and code compliance.

    Once a year is the minimum for most homes, and more often where water is hard. Homes without a softener or with heavy use may do better with a flush every six months.
