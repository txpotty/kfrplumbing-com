    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Urbandale

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin typically costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work usually ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    Constant running, a sounding alarm, sewage odors, gurgling drains and slow drainage are typical signs. Act fast to avoid a backup.

    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is built specifically to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve separate purposes.

    A sump pump removes groundwater or seepage, while an ejector pump handles sewage and wastewater from fixtures. Ejector pumps are designed to pass solids and need a sealed, vented basin.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that requires immediate attention.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can influence its lifespan. Annual inspections can help extend its longevity.

    Most ejector pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Flushing wipes or other non-flushable items can shorten that life considerably.
