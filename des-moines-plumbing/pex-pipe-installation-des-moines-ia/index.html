    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Des Moines, IA

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a tight seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can recover from kinks with a heat gun. It is our preferred choice for many full repipes.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Des Moines

    PEX is also a leading choice for hydronic heating systems, including radiant floor heating. Its ability to withstand high temperatures and resist corrosion makes it perfect for circulating hot water. If you are considering radiant heat for a specific area of your home or business in Des Moines, PEX is the pipe of choice.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Des Moines

    PEX-B is stiffer and more affordable, and it is joined with copper crimp rings or stainless steel cinch clamps over brass or poly fittings. It is a good option for smaller projects and budget-conscious repipes. Brands like Viega, Zurn and SharkBite offer reliable PEX-B systems. We use a go/no-go gauge on every crimp to verify a proper seal.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Des Moines, IA

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Many insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to sell and insure.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Des Moines, IA

    PEX repipes typically cost less than copper repipes because the material is less expensive and installs faster. The total depends on the number of bathrooms, the home's size and how accessible the attic and walls are. Drywall patching and texture matching are sometimes included or quoted separately. We give you a detailed estimate so you can compare PEX-A, PEX-B and copper options side by side.

    Frequently Asked Questions About PEX Pipe Installation in Des Moines

    A whole-home PEX repipe usually costs $4,500 to $12,000 depending on home size, bathroom count and access. Smaller jobs like a single bathroom remodel rough-in usually run $800 to $2,500.

    The timeline for PEX pipe installation depends on the size and complexity of the project. A small repair or extension might take a few hours, while a complete whole-home repipe for an average-sized house in Des Moines typically takes 2 to 5 days. We work efficiently to minimize disruption to your daily routine.

    PEX offers several advantages over copper, including lower material and installation costs, greater flexibility and resistance to corrosion and freezing. Unlike copper, PEX does not form pinhole leaks over time due to water chemistry, making it a more durable choice for many Des Moines homes. It also reduces water hammer noise.

    Multiple pinhole leaks, discolored water, low pressure throughout the house and gray polybutylene pipe are typical signs. Recurring slab leaks are also a strong reason to repipe overhead.

    PEX resists scale and corrosion, flexes with temperature changes and has fewer joints. Copper is very durable but can develop pinhole leaks in some water conditions and costs more to install.

    Properly installed PEX is expected to last many decades. Manufacturers often offer 25 year warranties when their pipe and fittings are used together.

    While PEX is simpler to install than copper, professional installation is highly recommended. Correct sizing, routing and secure connections are essential for preventing leaks and ensuring system longevity. Improper DIY installation can lead to costly water damage and code violations.

    PEX can be used for outdoor applications like irrigation lines or outdoor spigots, but it needs to be protected from direct UV light, which can degrade the material over time. When used outdoors, PEX should always be buried or otherwise shielded.
