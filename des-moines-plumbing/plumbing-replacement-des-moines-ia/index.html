    Water Softener Replacement in Des Moines, IA

    Hard water leads to mineral buildup in pipes and appliances. Water across much of Iowa comes from groundwater and runs very hard, so scale buildup in water heaters and fixtures is common. If your old water softener is no longer effectively removing minerals, or if it is over 15 years old, a replacement will protect your plumbing and improve water quality. We replace all types of water softeners, including salt-based and salt-free systems, sized for your household's water usage and installed correctly on your main water line to provide consistently soft water throughout your Des Moines home.

    Water Line Replacement in Des Moines, IA

    A leaking main water line can lead to significant water loss, high bills and foundation damage, which is a serious concern for homes on slab foundations. Our water line replacement services address issues like corroded pipes, leaks or low water pressure. We locate the leak, excavate the area if necessary and replace the damaged section or the entire line with durable PEX or copper piping. We make sure of a secure, leak-free connection from the meter to your home, bringing back reliable water flow.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Replacement in Des Moines

    The cost of plumbing replacement in Des Moines, IA varies widely depending on the type of service. A water heater replacement can range from $1,200 to $3,500, while a sewer line replacement might be $3,000 to $15,000 or more. Whole-home repiping can cost between $4,000 and $15,000. We provide detailed, clear estimates after an on-site assessment.

    For water heater replacements, we mainly recommend and install reliable brands such as Rheem, AO Smith, Bradford White and Navien. These manufacturers are known for their durability, energy efficiency and strong warranty coverage, providing long-term value for your Des Moines home.

    We install water heaters from Bradford White, Rheem and A.O. Smith, tankless units from Rinnai and Navien, and quality softeners and well pumps from proven manufacturers.

    Some simple fixture swaps are DIY friendly, but water heaters, well pumps and sewer lines involve gas, electrical and code requirements. A licensed plumber provides safety and compliance.

    Climate, freezes and soil type all affect how long plumbing lasts. A long heating season keeps water heaters and boilers working hard, while humid summers can cause sweating pipes and damp basements. Freeze-thaw cycles can damage pipes, and corrosive or shifting soils can speed up pipe degradation. Much of Iowa sits on rich, deep soil over glacial clay, which holds water and can push against basement walls and drain tile. Our replacement solutions are chosen to withstand these local conditions for lasting durability.

    Frequent leaks, rusty or discolored water, low pressure, recurring backups and equipment over 10 years old are typical signs. We can inspect and give you an honest assessment.
